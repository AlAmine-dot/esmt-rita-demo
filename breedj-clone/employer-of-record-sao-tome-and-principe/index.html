Statutory labor laws in Sao Tome and Principe

Compliance with Sao Tome and Principe’s labor laws is non-negotiable for businesses seeking a stable and lawful operation. Here are some key statutory elements to be aware of:

Employee Probation Period: Typically, the probationary period in Sao Tome and Principe lasts for up to 90 days. It’s crucial for employers to respect this timeframe to ensure that they are in full legal compliance, thereby reducing the risk of future disputes or litigation.

Annual Leave Entitlements: Sao Tome and Principe’s labor laws specify that employees are entitled to at least 20 working days of paid annual leave, exclusive of public holidays. Advanced planning around these leaves is essential to maintain optimal productivity and operational smoothness.

Maternity Leave: Generally, women in Sao Tome and Principe are eligible for a 12-week maternity leave that is fully paid. Proper accounting for this in your HR policies is a compliance prerequisite and contributes to employee satisfaction.

Paternity Leave: While the labor law provides a brief paternity leave window, often between 2 to 5 days, this duration can often be tailored through individual agreements between the employer and employee.

Sick Leave: Sao Tomean law permits full or partially paid sick leave, often up to 15 days. Understanding and planning for this provision is vital to ensure a harmonious work environment.
